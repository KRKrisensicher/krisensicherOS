# A.5.35 — Independent review of information security

## Purpose

This routine ensures that information security is not assessed only by those who operate it. Independent review creates distance, checks blind spots, makes deviations visible, and gives management and owners robust bases for decision-making.

The core is not an audit for audit’s sake, but repeatable review logic: What is reviewed independently, by whom, with which scope, which evidence, which findings, and which management handoff?

## Control objective in repository language

The organization plans and operates independent reviews of information security on a risk basis. Reviewing roles are sufficiently independent from the reviewed operations, results are documented traceably, findings are prioritized, and actions are tracked through to effectiveness review.

## Typical risks

- If teams assess their own security effectiveness alone, blind spots, conflicts of objectives, and habituation effects remain undiscovered.
- If reviews are only document-oriented, the actual operational routine does not become visible.
- If findings are not prioritized and tracked, audit theater arises without risk reduction.
- If reviewers are too close to implementation or interests, critical deviations can be softened.
- If management receives only result ratings instead of decision needs, resource and residual-risk questions remain unresolved.
- If external reviews are not prepared or evaluated, learning and improvement opportunities are lost.

## Triggers

- annual or risk-based review planning.
- material change in organization, architecture, service providers, systems, or threat situation.
- security event, near miss, vulnerability, or crisis exercise with structural finding.
- management question on effectiveness, maturity, resources, or residual risk.
- new regulatory, contractual, or customer requirement.
- completion of important actions, programs, or migrations.
- repeated deviations, overdue findings, or high exception rate.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **Management / Leadership:** commissions independent reviews, protects independence, and decides on resources, residual risks, and priorities.
- **ISMS owner:** coordinates review plan, scope, evidence provision, and action tracking without delegating away own responsibility.
- **Independent reviewers / internal audit / external reviewers:** review scope, execution, evidence, and effectiveness with sufficient distance.
- **Control Owner / Process Owner:** explain operations, provide evidence, assess findings, and implement actions.
- **Risk Owner:** classifies findings into risks, acceptances, and prioritization.
- **Legal / Data Protection:** review when review data, contracts, personal information, or report recipients are affected.
- **Audit/Evidence role:** supports evidence quality, sources, versions, and traceability.

## Implementation

### Minimum start

Goal: create an initial independent view of critical security areas.

1. Management and ISMS Owner name critical review areas: access, incident response, vulnerabilities, suppliers, backup, awareness, or data-protection handoffs.
2. For each area, review question, scope, reviewer, and time period are defined.
3. Reviewers must not solely review implementation for which they themselves are responsible.
4. The review considers at least documentation, an operational sample, and open exceptions.
5. Findings are documented with owner, risk, priority, action, and deadline.
6. Critical findings or unresolved conflicts of objectives go into management review.

Minimum evidence:

- review assignment with scope and reviewer,
- evidence list or sample plan,
- review note with findings,
- action log with owner and deadline,
- management decision for critical findings.

### Solid practice

Goal: independent reviews are planned and tracked on a risk basis.

1. A review plan prioritizes controls, processes, sites, systems, and service providers by risk and change.
2. Independence is defined per review: another team, internal audit, external specialist, or peer review.
3. Review methods combine interview, document review, sample, technical evidence, and walkthrough.
4. Findings are classified by impact, cause, affected assets, and decision need.
5. Actions are tracked through implementation and effectiveness review.
6. Repeat findings and systemic causes are reported separately to Management.
7. Lessons learned improve controls, training, architecture, supplier governance, or evidence design.

Strong evidence:

- risk-based review plan,
- independence and scope rationale,
- sample and review protocols,
- findings with cause, risk, and owner,
- action status and validation evidence,
- management review of critical or repeated findings.

### Advanced practice

Goal: independent review is part of the governance and improvement cycle.

1. Review planning uses risk register, incident trends, vulnerability situation, supplier risks, and management priorities.
2. Reviews are coordinated with internal audit, technical tests, tabletop exercises, architecture reviews, and evidence-pack reviews.
3. Reviewing roles have clear mandates, access to relevant evidence, and escalation paths.
4. Finding data shows patterns: root causes, control weaknesses, recurrence, overdue actions, and resource conflicts.
5. Action validation reviews not only closure, but actual effectiveness.
6. Management receives decision-capable reports with residual risks, options, and human gates.

## Routine flow

1. **Identify review need:** interval, risk change, incident, management question, audit finding, or completed action.
2. **Define scope and independence:** What is reviewed, who reviews, which conflicts of interest must be avoided?
3. **Plan review approach:** define review questions, samples, evidence sources, interviews, and technical evidence.
4. **Conduct:** understand operations, review evidence, record deviations and good practice.
5. **Assess findings:** define cause, risk, impact, owner, priority, and decision need.
6. **Agree actions:** document correction, risk treatment, exception, acceptance, or management decision.
7. **Track:** manage deadlines, status, obstacles, and escalations.
8. **Validate effectiveness:** review sample, test, re-review, or evidence.
9. **Learn:** feed results back into review plan, controls, training, and management review.

## Decisions

- Which security areas need independent review and how often?
- Which form of independence is sufficient for the respective scope?
- Which findings can be solved operationally, and which need management decision?
- When are external specialists or internal audit needed?
- Which evidence is sufficient to confirm effectiveness of an action?
- How are conflicts of interest, reporting pressure, or disputed findings handled?

## Evidence

### Strong evidence

- approved review plan with risk relation,
- review assignment with scope, method, and reviewer,
- evidence of independence consideration,
- sample list and reviewed evidence sources,
- finding register with cause, risk, owner, and deadline,
- validation evidence after action closure,
- management decision on critical findings, exceptions, or resources.

### Weak evidence

- general audit planning without risk relation,
- self-assessment by the reviewed team without independent sample,
- findings without owner or deadline,
- traffic-light report without causes and decision need,
- closed actions without effectiveness evidence,
- external review reports without internal evaluation.

### Evidence gaps

- no review plan or no prioritization,
- reviewer reviews exclusively own work,
- no samples or operational evidence,
- findings are not tracked,
- management does not see critical residual risks,
- data-protection/legal questions relating to review data remain unclear.

## Effectiveness review

Review questions:

- Does the review plan cover the most important risks, changes, and critical controls?
- Is reviewer independence traceable for the scope?
- Do reviews check actual execution and effectiveness, not only documents?
- Are findings prioritized, assigned to an owner, and tracked through validation?
- Are repeated or systemic weaknesses made visible?
- Does Management receive clear decisions instead of mere review reports?

Possible metrics:

- coverage of risk-critical review areas,
- share of findings with owner and deadline,
- overdue actions,
- repeated findings,
- validated action rate,
- time from finding to management decision,
- share of reviews with documented independence consideration.

## BSIG/NIS2 connection point

Independent review is connectable to NIS2-oriented governance, risk management, management oversight, effectiveness review, incident lessons learned, and supply-chain governance. The specific connection should be defined in an organization-specific way in the requirements register, review plan, and management review.

This artifact does not replace legal interpretation, certification audit, or binding assessment of regulatory obligations.

## Boundaries

- No certification, conformity, or security guarantee.
- No replacement for internal audit, external review, penetration test, or legal advice.
- No ISO 27002 text or standards-substitute wording.
- No publication or external sharing of review reports without approval.
- No real customer, personal, security, or secret data in public examples.

## Handoffs

- **Management handoff:** review assignment, critical findings, resource need, risk acceptance, prioritization.
- **Internal review/Audit handoff:** review methodology, samples, report, tracking.
- **Risk handoff:** findings with risk change, acceptance need, or action prioritization.
- **IT/Security handoff:** technical deviations, control design, action implementation.
- **Legal/Data Protection handoff:** personal review data, contracts, external reviewers, report recipients, confidentiality.
- **Incident/BCM handoff:** findings from incidents, exercises, or crisis routines.
- **Evidence handoff:** missing or weak evidence, controls that cannot be reviewed.

## Typical mistakes

- Review is confused with self-assessment.
- Review plan follows calendar logic instead of risk and change situation.
- Findings are worded softly so that no decision becomes necessary.
- Actions are closed as soon as a document has been created.
- External reports are filed but not translated into actions and risks.
- Independence is claimed, but conflicts of interest are not considered.
- Management receives traffic lights without concrete decision points.

## Fictional mini example

After a cloud migration, a fictional company has access control reviewed independently. A reviewer from another area receives the scope, sample plan, and access to approval tickets. The review shows that external admin access is documented but not reviewed on time. The Cloud Owner creates an action, the ISMS Owner sets a follow-up, and Management decides to prioritize monthly reviews for three critical systems.

Evidence:

- review assignment with independent reviewer role,
- sample list,
- finding on external admin access,
- action ticket with owner and deadline,
- management decision on review frequency,
- validation note after the first monthly review.
