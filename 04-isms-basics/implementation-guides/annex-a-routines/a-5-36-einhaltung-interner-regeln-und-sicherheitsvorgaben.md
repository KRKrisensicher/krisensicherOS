# A.5.36 — Compliance with internal rules and security requirements

## Purpose

This routine ensures that internal security rules are not only published, but understood, applied, reviewed, and improved in everyday work. It makes deviations visible, distinguishes between mistakes, conflicting objectives, and deliberate exceptions, and routes relevant issues to the right decision point.

The core is not control for its own sake, but a fair and effective governance routine: Which rules apply, who reviews compliance, how are deviations handled, and when are management, HR, data protection, or legal needed?

## Control objective in repository language

The organisation operates a traceable routine for communicating internal information security rules, translating them into processes, reviewing them on a risk basis, and correcting, escalating, or improving them when deviations occur. Exceptions, violations, and recurring patterns are tracked in an evidence-ready way.

## Typical risks

- If security rules only exist on the intranet but are not translated into work processes, teams act according to habit rather than requirement.
- If compliance is never reviewed, deviations remain invisible and risks grow unnoticed.
- If violations are sanctioned across the board instead of being understood by cause, learning opportunities and trust are lost.
- If exceptions are tolerated informally, permanent special paths emerge without a risk decision.
- If technical requirements are not connected with operations, procurement, HR, or development, security requirements remain practically ineffective.
- If reviews contain personal evaluations without data protection/HR handoff, new governance risks arise.

## Triggers

- new or changed policy, security requirement, operating rule, or work instruction.
- new system, process, provider, site, team, or work model.
- audit finding, internal review, vulnerability, incident, or recurring user error.
- planned control review, sample check, or management question.
- exception request, rule conflict, or requirement that cannot be implemented.
- role change, onboarding, offboarding, or new critical task.
- technical deviation from monitoring, configuration review, or access review.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **Policy Owner / ISMS Owner:** defines minimum requirements, review logic, exceptions, and escalation paths.
- **Control Owner / Process Owner:** translates rules into concrete workflows and reviews execution.
- **Managers:** ensure implementation in the team and address recurring deviations.
- **IT / Platform Owner:** implements technical requirements, baselines, configurations, and evidence.
- **HR / People function:** supports training, work-related measures, and fair handling of violations.
- **Data protection / Legal:** review personal evaluations, employment-law questions, contracts, and sanction topics.
- **Internal review / Audit:** reviews compliance, evidence, and action tracking independently or by sampling.
- **Management:** decides on conflicting objectives, resources, risk acceptance, and repeated critical deviations.

## Implementation

### Minimum start

Goal: The most important security rules are known, reviewable, and equipped with a deviation path.

1. The organisation names the most important internal security rules: access, data classification, tool use, incident reporting, mobile work, suppliers, change, backup, or development.
2. Each critical rule has an owner and an affected target group.
3. Rules are translated into simple work instructions, checklists, or process steps.
4. At least one regular sample check reviews actual compliance in a critical area.
5. Deviations are documented with cause, risk, action, and owner.
6. Exceptions require justification, duration, review date, and an appropriate decision.

Minimum evidence:

- rule overview with owner and target group,
- communication or training evidence,
- sample check record,
- deviation or exception log,
- management decision for critical or permanent deviation.

### Solid practice

Goal: Rule compliance is reviewed in a risk-based, fair, and improvement-oriented way.

1. Policies and requirements are linked to processes, controls, roles, and evidence sources.
2. Review frequency and sample size are based on risk, criticality, and deviation history.
3. Deviations are distinguished by cause: lack of knowledge, unclear rule, process obstacle, tool problem, conflicting objective, exception, deliberate violation.
4. Corrective actions are tracked and reviewed for effectiveness.
5. Recurring patterns lead to adjustment of the rule, training, tooling, or management decision.
6. Personal evaluations are clarified with data protection/HR in advance.
7. Results feed into ISMS review, risk register, and management review.

Strong evidence:

- policy/control mapping,
- risk-based review plan,
- sample checks with result and sources,
- deviation register with cause and action,
- exception decisions with expiry date,
- effectiveness review after correction,
- management review of systemic problems.

### Advanced practice

Goal: Compliance with internal requirements is integrated into operations, tooling, and learning cycles.

1. Technical controls, configuration checks, ticket workflows, and GRC registers provide compliance signals automatically or semi-automatically.
2. Critical rules are embedded in templates, pipelines, procurement, IAM, change, and onboarding.
3. Deviations are triaged in real time or regularly and prioritised by risk.
4. Dashboards show trends: repeated rule breaches, exceptions, overdue actions, and structural causes.
5. Lessons learned from incidents, audits, and reviews lead to better rules, training, and process designs.
6. Management receives concrete options: change the rule, improve tooling, provide resources, accept risk, or trigger escalation.

## Routine flow

1. **Rule or review need arises:** new requirement, change, finding, incident, sample check, or management question.
2. **Clarify scope:** determine affected processes, roles, systems, sites, providers, and data classes.
3. **Operationalise the rule:** define concrete expectation, evidence source, review question, and exception path.
4. **Communicate and enable:** inform the target group, conduct training or management briefing.
5. **Review compliance:** perform sample check, technical review, review, interview, or evidence-pack check.
6. **Assess deviation:** determine cause, risk, impact, and decision need.
7. **Handle:** define correction, training, process change, technical measure, exception, or escalation.
8. **Track:** review action and exception through closure and effectiveness.
9. **Improve:** adapt rules, processes, controls, or training.

## Decisions

- Which internal security rules are critical enough for regular review?
- Which evidence shows actual compliance rather than mere acknowledgement?
- Which deviations may be corrected operationally, and which require management decision?
- When is an exception appropriate, and who may approve it?
- Which personal evaluations are permissible and clarified for compliance reviews?
- When must a rule be changed because it is unclear, unrealistic, or contradictory?
- How are repeated violations handled fairly, effectively, and proportionately?

## Evidence

### Strong evidence

- current rule overview with owner, target group, and review date,
- mapping of rules to processes, controls, and evidence,
- risk-based sample check records,
- technical review or configuration evidence,
- deviation log with cause, risk, action, and owner,
- exception decisions with duration and review date,
- evidence of corrected deviations and effectiveness review,
- management decision on conflicting objectives or systemic problems.

### Weak evidence

- policy on the intranet without communication or implementation evidence,
- training participation rate without connection to behaviour or process,
- checklist without sample check,
- technical reports without assessment or owner,
- verbally tolerated exception,
- traffic-light status without deviation cause and decision.

### Evidence gaps

- no overview of applicable security rules,
- unclear owners for policies or controls,
- no review planning,
- deviations without action,
- exceptions without expiry date,
- personal evaluations without data protection/HR handoff,
- management does not see repeated critical deviations.

## Effectiveness review

Review questions:

- Do affected roles know the security rules and work paths relevant to them?
- Are critical rules built into processes, tools, or checklists?
- Are compliance and exceptions reviewed on a risk basis?
- Do deviations lead to correction, learning, or decision?
- Are recurring patterns and conflicting objectives made visible?
- Are personal reviews clarified with data protection/HR?
- Does management receive decision-ready information instead of mere rule violations?

Possible metrics:

- share of critical rules with owner and review date,
- sample checks performed according to plan,
- open deviations by criticality,
- overdue actions,
- exception rate and overdue exceptions,
- repeated deviations per process or rule,
- time from critical deviation to decision.

## BSIG/NIS2 connection point

Compliance with internal rules and security requirements is a connection point for NIS2-oriented governance, risk management, cyber hygiene, management oversight, incident capability, training, and supply-chain control. The specific connection should be assessed for the organisation in the requirements register, ISMS review, and management review.

This artefact does not replace legal assessment of employment-law, data-protection, or regulatory questions and does not establish a conformity statement.

## Boundaries

- No legal, data protection, or employment-law advice.
- No certification, conformity, or security guarantee.
- No substitute for internal audit, HR process, or disciplinary procedure.
- No ISO 27002 text or substitute wording for the standard.
- No real personal, customer, contract, or secret data in public examples.
- No personal performance or behaviour monitoring without responsible clarification.

## Handoffs

- **Management handoff:** permanent exceptions, critical deviations, lack of resources, unrealistic requirements, risk acceptance.
- **HR handoff:** training, repeated personal deviations, fair handling, work-related measures.
- **Data protection handoff:** personal reviews, monitoring, access evaluations, proximity to behaviour monitoring.
- **Legal handoff:** contract, employment-law, regulatory, or external communication questions.
- **IT/Security handoff:** technical deviations, baselines, configurations, access, monitoring.
- **Risk handoff:** deviations with risk change or acceptance need.
- **Audit/Evidence handoff:** missing evidence, weak sample checks, unclear findings.
- **Awareness handoff:** lack of knowledge, user error, or rule changes requiring training.

## Typical mistakes

- Policies are published but not translated into processes.
- Compliance is reviewed only during audits.
- Every deviation is assessed as misconduct although the rule or process is unclear.
- Exceptions become permanent and invisible.
- Technical reports are collected, but no one assesses risk or cause.
- Personal evaluations run without data protection and HR clarification.
- Management receives violation counts, but no options for decision.

## Fictional mini example

A fictional mid-sized company introduces an internal rule requiring external file shares to be time-limited and documented with an owner. Three months later, the ISMS Owner reviews a sample. Several shares have no end date because the business units did not know a simple flow. Instead of only reprimanding them, the sharing process in the collaboration tool is adjusted, a short note is sent to team leads, and a time-limited exception for two customer projects is brought into management review.

Evidence:

- rule overview with owner,
- sample check record,
- deviation log with cause,
- ticket for tool adjustment,
- communication evidence to team leads,
- management decision on time-limited exceptions.
