# A.7.7 — Clear desks and screens

## Purpose

Clear desks and protected screens reduce the risk that information is casually disclosed, photographed, taken away, mixed up or viewed by unauthorised persons in everyday work. The core is not desk aesthetics, but a simple, lived routine for sensitive information at physical and digital workplaces.

## Control objective in repository language

The organisation operates an everyday routine that appropriately protects confidential documents, notes, printouts, data media, mobile devices and screen contents in office, home office, customer, travel and shared-desk situations. The routine connects data classes, work context, role behaviour, technical settings, awareness, review and exception handling.

## Typical risks

- If confidential documents are left visible, visitors, service providers or uninvolved employees may view or photograph information.
- If screens remain unlocked and unattended, information may be read, changed or misused through active sessions.
- If printouts, notes or data media are not disposed of in a controlled way, leakage and mix-up risks arise.
- If shared desks, meeting rooms or home office are not considered, the routine remains limited to classic office workplaces.
- If rules are too strict or impractical, they are ignored and do not create robust evidence.

## Triggers

- Introduction or change of data classes, confidentiality rules or working models.
- New office space, shared desk, customer area, co-working, home office or travel activity.
- Security event, discovery of confidential documents, unattended device or misrouting through printouts.
- Onboarding, role change or new activity with sensitive information.
- Introduction of new devices, printers, meeting technology or screen-lock settings.
- Regular awareness, workplace or office review.
- Audit finding, customer requirement or management decision on work culture and protection needs.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **Employees and external staff:** protect documents, devices and screen contents in their own work context.
- **Managers / Team Leads:** make expectations, everyday practicability and follow-up visible in the team.
- **ISMS Owner / Security role:** defines minimum rules, risk reference, awareness and review logic.
- **IT / Endpoint Owner:** implements screen lock, printer logic, device protection and minimum technical configurations.
- **Facility / Office Management:** provides suitable disposal, storage and workplace options.
- **HR / People function:** anchors rules in onboarding and role changes.
- **Data protection / Legal:** reviews personal controls, employee context, customer or contract requirements.

## Implementation

### Minimum start

Goal: simple, understandable rules protect the most common everyday situations.

1. The organisation defines which information must not remain openly visible at the workplace.
2. Basic rules are formulated briefly: put documents away, collect confidential printouts, store data media securely, lock the screen, clear meeting rooms.
3. Automatic screen locks are configured for managed devices.
4. A secure disposal option exists for paper and data media.
5. New people receive the rules during onboarding; critical roles receive short additional guidance.
6. A light review checks samples or events without personal exposure.

Minimum evidence:

- Short workplace/screen rule,
- technical setting for screen lock,
- onboarding or awareness evidence,
- disposal or storage option,
- review or finding note with measure.

### Solid practice

Goal: the routine is adapted to data classes, workplaces and roles.

1. Rules are made concrete by work context: office, shared desk, meeting room, reception, home office, travel, customer appointment.
2. Printers, scanners and multifunction devices are operated so confidential printouts are not unnecessarily left exposed.
3. Teams with sensitive data receive practice-oriented scenarios and clear stop points.
4. Workplace and meeting-room checks are designed as a culture and improvement routine, not as a surveillance ritual.
5. Findings lead to root cause analysis: missing cabinets, unclear printer logic, poor rooms, impractical rules, missing awareness.
6. A data protection/legal handoff is triggered when personal controls, evaluations or disciplinary questions arise.
7. Results flow into awareness, facility, IT and management reviews.

Strong evidence:

- Context-related rules or quick-reference card,
- technical configurations for lock and printing,
- evidence of secure disposal,
- awareness or team briefing,
- anonymised review findings with measures,
- management decision for resource needs such as cabinets, privacy filters or printer conversion.

### Advanced practice

Goal: workplace and screen protection is integrated into work design, endpoint management and security culture.

1. Endpoint and MDM configurations enforce lock times, device protection and remote-lock capability on a risk basis.
2. Roles with high protection needs use privacy filters, secure meeting rooms, follow-me print or reduced paper processes.
3. Office, home office and travel requirements are connected with data classification and customer requirements.
4. Recurring findings are treated as a design problem: storage surfaces, printer locations, room design, work pressure, process media breaks.
5. Exercises or short campaigns test reporting and correction capability without normalising individual surveillance.
6. Management receives decision-ready information on resources, work models and residual risks.

## Routine flow

1. **Determine work context:** which information is created or used at the workplace?
2. **Apply rules:** handle documents, data media, devices and screens according to protection need.
3. **Support with technology:** provide lock times, device protection, print release and secure disposal.
4. **Check everyday practice:** use samples, self-checks, team routines or findings from incidents.
5. **Handle deviation:** clarify cause, define measure and involve Data Protection/Legal or Management if needed.
6. **Learn:** improve awareness, workplace design, printing processes or technical settings.
7. **Review:** regularly check rules and evidence against work model, data classes and real use.

## Decisions

- Which data classes or work contexts need special workplace rules?
- Which lock times and printer functions are secure while still enabling work?
- Where are behavioural rules insufficient, requiring cabinets, privacy filters, secure rooms or technical changes?
- How are home office, travel, co-working and customer appointments covered appropriately?
- Which form of review is effective without monitoring employees inappropriately?
- When does a finding become an incident, data protection topic or management item?

## Evidence

### Strong evidence

- Current rule for workplace, screen, paper and data media protection,
- technical evidence for screen lock, device protection or print release,
- onboarding and awareness evidence,
- secure disposal and storage evidence,
- anonymised review or sample results with measures,
- incident or finding evidence with lessons learned,
- management decision for resource or objective conflicts.

### Weak evidence

- Poster or policy without transfer into everyday work,
- screen lock only as a recommendation without technical implementation,
- workplace check without measure tracking,
- training slide without reference to data classes or workplaces,
- blanket statement “everyone clears up themselves” without owner or review.

### Evidence gaps

- Confidential printouts regularly remain at printers,
- shared desks and meeting rooms are not checked,
- home office or travel are completely missing,
- no secure disposal for paper or data media,
- personal controls without data protection/legal clarification,
- recurring findings without root cause analysis.

## Effectiveness review

Review questions:

- Are rules short and concrete enough for employees to apply in everyday work?
- Does technology support the routine, especially screen lock and printing processes?
- Are confidential documents and data media stored or disposed of securely?
- Are shared desks, meeting rooms, home office and travel covered?
- Do findings lead to improvements instead of only reminders?
- Are personal evaluations and controls appropriately clarified?

Possible metrics:

- Devices with enforced screen lock,
- findings from workplace/room checks,
- open measures for printers, cabinets or disposal,
- awareness coverage for critical roles,
- incidents with exposed documents or unlocked devices,
- overdue measures from recurring findings.

## BSIG/NIS2 connection point

Clear desks and screens are a connection point for NIS2-oriented topics such as cyber hygiene, access protection, awareness, protection of information in operations and incident prevention. The concrete connection should be assessed organisation-specifically through data classes, risk analysis, working models and the requirements register.

This artefact does not replace legal, data protection, employment-law or co-determination-related review.

## Boundaries

- This artefact is not a complete data protection, home office or workplace policy.
- It does not replace employment-law or co-determination-related assessment of controls.
- It does not guarantee confidentiality if the work environment or technology remain unsuitable.
- It contains no ISO 27002 text or certification promise.
- Public examples remain fictional and without real personal or organisational data.

## Handoffs

- **IT handoff:** screen lock, endpoint/MDM configuration, print release, device protection, remote lock.
- **Facility / Office handoff:** cabinets, privacy filters, printer locations, secure disposal, meeting rooms.
- **HR / Awareness handoff:** onboarding, role changes, team briefings, external staff.
- **Data protection / Legal handoff:** personal checks, employee context, customer requirements, incident assessment.
- **Incident handoff:** lost documents, disclosed information, unattended devices, unauthorised viewing.
- **Management handoff:** resource needs, impractical work environment, conflicts between work capability and protection needs.
- **Audit / Evidence handoff:** missing technical evidence, unclear rules or untracked findings.

## Typical mistakes

- The rule is formulated as an orderliness topic instead of protection of sensitive information.
- Screen lock is expected but not technically enforced.
- Printers and meeting rooms are forgotten.
- Home office and travel remain outside scope.
- Checks are understood as individual control and create resistance instead of improvement.
- Findings lead to reminders, but not to better storage, printing processes or working conditions.

## Fictional mini example

A fictional consulting company uses shared desks. During an anonymised office check, confidential workshop printouts are found in a meeting room. The ISMS Owner clarifies secure paper disposal with Facility and print release at the device with IT. Team Leads add a short rule to project onboarding: printouts only when needed, collect immediately, check meeting rooms at the end.

Evidence:

- Anonymised finding note,
- measure ticket for print release,
- evidence of secure disposal containers,
- updated project onboarding rule,
- review note after four weeks.
