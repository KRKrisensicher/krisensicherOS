# A.6.1 — Security screening before employment or engagement

## Purpose

Security screening before employment or engagement helps fill trust-relevant roles deliberately and clarify obvious suitability, integrity, or role conflicts before access to information requiring protection is granted.

The core is a proportionate, legally clarified, and role-based screening routine — not blanket distrust and not unchecked data collection.

## Control objective in repository language

The organisation defines for which roles which security screening is appropriate, permissible, and necessary before an activity begins. The routine connects role risk, HR/procurement process, data protection/legal review, decision, documentation, and exception handling.

## Typical risks

- If people with access to critical systems start without suitable prior screening, unresolved conflicts of interest or deception may remain undetected.
- If screening is performed across the board and without legal/data protection clarification, data protection, employment-law, or reputational risks arise.
- If external roles are not included, provider accesses receive no comparable trust basis.
- If results are not documented in a decision-ready way, exceptions and residual risks remain unclear.
- If security screening and access assignment run separately, people may work productively before necessary clarifications are complete.

## Triggers

- planned hiring, engagement, or deployment of external staff.
- filling a role with privileged access, sensitive data, security, finance, HR, operations, or development responsibility.
- move into a more critical role.
- new provider with people accessing systems, sites, or information.
- changed risk analysis, role classification, or legal framework review.
- audit finding, event, or management decision on trust screening.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **HR / People function:** integrates screening logic into recruiting, onboarding, and role changes.
- **Hiring Manager / Manager:** assesses role need, criticality, and start prerequisites.
- **ISMS Owner / Security role:** defines risk-based criteria, minimum requirements, and escalation points.
- **Legal / Data protection:** reviews permissibility, proportionality, information obligations, retention, and external evidence.
- **Procurement / Supplier management:** ensures requirements for provider roles and evidence in the engagement process.
- **Management:** decides on exceptions, residual risks, or resource conflicts.

## Implementation

### Minimum start

Goal: Critical roles do not start without clarified security screening.

1. The organisation names roles for which security screening may be necessary before start.
2. HR, ISMS Owner, and Legal/Data protection clarify which screening is permissible and appropriate for these roles.
3. The recruiting or engagement process contains a checkpoint before access assignment.
4. Results are documented minimally: screening performed, decision, date, role, responsible body.
5. Exceptions are time-limited, justified, and decided before productive access.

Minimum evidence:

- role list with screening category,
- clarified screening logic with legal/data protection note,
- check in the onboarding or engagement process,
- decision before access assignment,
- exception decision with review date.

### Solid practice

Goal: Security screening is risk-based, repeatable, and connected with access.

1. Roles are classified by access, data class, privileges, external impact, and dependency.
2. Permissible evidence, responsible parties, deadlines, and retention rules are defined for each screening category.
3. External staff and provider roles are included in the same role logic.
4. Start approval, access assignment, and training/commitment steps are linked with each other.
5. Incomplete screenings trigger controlled restriction, escalation, or postponement of the start.
6. The process is regularly improved based on sample checks and findings.

Strong evidence:

- role and screening categories,
- process evidence with gate before access assignment,
- approval or escalation decision,
- provider requirement for deployed people,
- sample review,
- documented exceptions with expiry date.

### Advanced practice

Goal: Trust screening is part of the workforce, supplier, and access governance model.

1. HR system, supplier onboarding, and IAM start approvals use the same role and risk categories.
2. Critical roles receive additional human gates before privileged access.
3. Changes to role risk or legal framework lead to review of the screening logic.
4. Metrics show open screenings, exceptions, delayed start approvals, and critical roles without evidence.
5. Management assesses conflicting objectives between fast staffing, protection needs, and legally permissible screening.

## Routine flow

1. **Role need arises:** hiring, engagement, or role change.
2. **Determine criticality:** assess access, data, privileges, site or process responsibility.
3. **Derive screening requirement:** use only clarified, proportionate, and role-based screening.
4. **Perform screening or obtain evidence:** HR, provider, or authorised body processes the check.
5. **Make decision:** approve, restrict, postpone, or escalate start.
6. **Link access:** productive access occurs only after required approval or documented exception.
7. **Store evidence:** document only necessary metadata and decisions according to clarified retention.
8. **Review:** transfer sample checks, exceptions, and findings into process improvements.

## Decisions

- Which roles are relevant for screening, and why?
- Which screenings are appropriate, permissible, and necessary for which role?
- Which information may be stored, and for how long?
- What happens if a screening is not completed in time?
- Who may approve an exception before start or access?
- Which requirements apply to external staff and providers?

## Evidence

### Strong evidence

- role classification with screening category,
- documented legal/data protection clarification of screening logic,
- onboarding or engagement check with decision before access,
- evidence that access assignment is linked to approval,
- exception with reason, duration, and risk decision,
- sample check record with corrective actions.

### Weak evidence

- blanket statement “background checks are performed” without role reference,
- copies of sensitive evidence without clarified necessity,
- HR checklist without connection to access,
- provider contract without evidence for deployed people,
- old screening requirement without review date.

### Evidence gaps

- critical roles without defined screening category,
- access is granted before necessary screening is completed,
- external roles are missing from the process,
- no exception decision for incomplete screening,
- no data protection/legal clarification of screening practice.

## Effectiveness review

Review questions:

- Are screening-relevant roles classified traceably?
- Has the screening logic been reviewed by humans for legal and data protection aspects?
- Is productive access linked to the required start approval?
- Are external staff and provider roles covered?
- Are exceptions time-limited and decided?
- Do sample checks lead to process corrections?

Possible metrics:

- critical roles with defined screening category,
- starts with completed screening before access,
- open or delayed screenings,
- exceptions by role and duration,
- external roles without complete evidence,
- findings from sample checks.

## BSIG/NIS2 connection point

Security screening before activities begin is a connection point for NIS2-oriented governance, risk management, access protection, supply-chain control, and security culture. The specific scope should be assessed through the role model, requirements register, and human review.

This artefact does not replace employment-law, data-protection, or other legal assessment.

## Boundaries

- No legal or data protection advice and no recommendation on concrete screening types in individual cases.
- No blanket screening of all people without role and proportionality reference.
- No storage of sensitive evidence without clarified necessity and retention.
- No certification, conformity, or security guarantee.
- No ISO 27002 text or real personal data.

## Handoffs

- **HR handoff:** recruiting, onboarding, role change, start approval, and retention logic.
- **Legal/Data protection handoff:** permissibility, proportionality, information obligations, evidence types, and retention.
- **Access handoff:** access assignment, privileged rights, and start restrictions.
- **Supplier handoff:** external people, subcontractors, evidence, and contract reference.
- **Management handoff:** exceptions, hard-to-fill roles, residual risk, or conflicting objective between staffing and protection need.
- **Audit/Evidence handoff:** missing screening categories, unclear decisions, or non-traceable exceptions.

## Typical mistakes

- Security screening is applied across the board instead of role-based.
- HR performs checks, but IT grants access independently of them.
- External staff are not considered equivalently.
- Too many personal details are stored although a yes/no decision would be sufficient.
- Exceptions emerge informally to meet a start date.
- The process is never reviewed against current roles, risks, and legal situation.

## Fictional mini example

A fictional company fills an administrator role for central identity services. HR recognises the role as relevant for screening; Legal and Data protection have already approved the permissible screening logic. The check is completed before start, and onboarding documents only the decision “approved on date”. The IAM approval refers to this status. For an external specialist who is to support at short notice, a time-limited exception with restricted access and management approval is documented.

Evidence:

- role classification “critical admin role”,
- documented screening gate in onboarding,
- start approval before IAM access,
- exception decision for external specialist,
- review date for the exception.
