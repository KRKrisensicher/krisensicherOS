# A.5.4 — Management responsibility in security operations

## Purpose

Management responsibility in security operations ensures that information security is not treated as a purely specialist or IT task. Managers must steer security objectives, resources, priorities and behavioral expectations so that operational roles are able to work and conflicts of objectives are decided.

## Control objective in repository language

The organization operates a management routine in which responsible persons support security requirements in their areas, make decisions, escalate deviations and follow up implementation in day-to-day work, leadership and review.

## Typical risks

- If management only delegates information security, priority, resources and decision power are missing.
- If managers do not translate security requirements into teamwork, rules remain without consequence.
- If conflicts between revenue, availability, speed and security are not decided, shadow decisions emerge.
- If security behavior is not managed, reporting paths, access requirements or change rules are bypassed.
- If management reviews only receive reports, risks and measures remain without resolution.
- If external communication or authority contact is unclear, an event can escalate in an uncoordinated way.

## Triggers

- management review, risk review, budget planning or strategy change.
- security event, near miss, crisis exercise or lessons learned.
- critical audit finding, overdue measure or persistent residual risk.
- introduction of new services, working models, suppliers or technologies.
- resource bottleneck, priority conflict or deviation from security guardrails.
- new manager, reorganization or changed areas of responsibility.
- planned review of security objectives and leadership expectations.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **Top management / executive management:** sets security priorities, decides resources, risk acceptance and escalations.
- **Area and line management:** translates security requirements into team objectives, routines and behavior.
- **ISMS owner / security role:** prepares situation picture, decision needs, risks and effectiveness information.
- **Risk owner:** brings risk decisions and measures status into the management routine.
- **Process and asset owners:** provide implementation status, deviations and business conflicts of objectives.
- **Incident/BCM roles:** escalate events, exercise results and crisis decisions.
- **Legal / data protection / communications:** support legal, personal-data or external communication questions.

## Implementation

### Minimum start

Objective: Management makes visible security decisions instead of only receiving reports.

1. The organization names which management body makes security decisions.
2. The ISMS owner and relevant owners prepare a short decision agenda: top risks, open measures, exceptions, resource needs.
3. Managers receive clear expectations on reporting paths, access, policy adherence and escalation.
4. Critical conflicts of objectives are minuted and decided or given a follow-up date.
5. Decisions are fed back into the measures log, risk register or routines.
6. A next review date is defined.

Minimum evidence:

- management review agenda or decision paper,
- minutes with resolutions and owners,
- measures or risk log with management connection,
- documented leadership expectations,
- follow-up of open decisions.

### Solid practice

Objective: Management responsibility is integrated into leadership, planning and reviews.

1. Security objectives are connected with area objectives, risk appetite and measures portfolio.
2. Managers review relevant security routines in their teams: onboarding, access, reporting path, suppliers, change.
3. Management reviews consider not only metrics, but decisions, trends, exceptions and learning points.
4. Resource and capacity gaps are documented as a risk or management decision.
5. Lessons learned from incidents, exercises and audits lead to concrete resolutions.
6. New managers are briefed on security responsibility and escalation paths.

### Advanced practice

Objective: Management steers security capability as part of organizational leadership.

1. Security situation, risk development, ability to implement measures and resilience are reported regularly in a decision-oriented way.
2. Area managers have their own security routines and provide evidence from operations.
3. Management decisions are connected with budget, prioritization, acceptance boundaries and effectiveness review.
4. Tabletop exercises test decision capability for incident, crisis communication, supplier outage or data leakage.
5. Culture and behavior indicators complement technical metrics.
6. Strategic changes trigger early security and resilience assessments.

## Routine flow

1. **Prepare situation picture:** collect risks, measures, incidents, findings, exceptions and resource needs.
2. **Formulate decision need:** What must management decide, accept, prioritize or escalate?
3. **Hold management meeting:** present facts, options, residual risks and conflicts of objectives concisely.
4. **Pass resolution:** document owner, deadline, resources, acceptance or rejection.
5. **Feed back into operations:** update measures log, risk register, policies, routines and communication.
6. **Connect leadership:** area managers translate resolutions into team routines.
7. **Follow up:** review status, effectiveness and open points in the next review.
8. **Escalate:** handle unacceptable delays, new risks or external communication needs separately.

## Decisions

- Which management body decides security risks and resources?
- Which security objectives and tolerance limits apply to areas and processes?
- Which residual risks may be accepted operationally, and which need management?
- Which measures are prioritized, postponed or consciously not implemented?
- How are managers made accountable for security behavior and reporting paths?
- When are legal, data protection, communications, BCM or authority contact involved?

## Evidence

### Strong evidence

- management review minutes with concrete resolutions,
- decision papers with options, risk and resource needs,
- risk register entries with management acceptance or escalation,
- measures log with owner, deadline and follow-up,
- evidence from leadership communication or area routines,
- exercise or incident lessons learned with management measures,
- follow-ups on open conflicts of objectives.

### Weak evidence

- presentation with metrics but without decision,
- general management statement without implementation,
- minutes without owner or deadlines,
- security objectives without area connection,
- verbal prioritization without evidence.

### Evidence gaps

- critical risks without management decision,
- measures overdue without escalation,
- resource shortage not documented as a risk,
- managers do not know their security role,
- incident lessons learned without resolution or follow-up,
- conflicts of objectives are sat out operationally.

## Effectiveness review

Review questions:

- Are real decisions made in management review?
- Are owners, deadlines and resources for resolutions traceable?
- Are overdue risks and measures escalated?
- Do managers translate security requirements into their areas?
- Do incidents, exercises and audit findings lead to management measures?
- Are risk acceptances conscious, time-limited and reviewable?

Possible metrics:

- open management decisions,
- overdue measures with management relevance,
- accepted residual risks with follow-up date,
- share of reviews with documented resolutions,
- resource gaps by security domain,
- measures from incidents or exercises with implementation status.

## BSIG/NIS2 connection point

Management responsibility is connectable to NIS2-oriented governance, risk management, management oversight, incident capability, business continuity and training of responsible persons. For affected organizations, the concrete connection should be assessed in the requirements register, in management reviews and in risk management.

This artifact does not replace legal assessment of management duties or applicability.

## Boundaries

- No legal advice on duties of corporate bodies, liability or reporting obligations.
- No certification or conformity assurance.
- No decision by templates or aids instead of management.
- No false sense of security through metrics without resolution.
- No complete crisis or communication rule.

## Handoffs

- **Risk handoff:** risk acceptance, prioritization, residual risk and escalation.
- **ISMS handoff:** review agenda, measures log, effectiveness review and evidence pack.
- **HR/managers handoff:** role understanding, training, onboarding of new managers.
- **Incident/BCM handoff:** event decisions, crisis roles, restart, lessons learned.
- **Legal/data protection/communications handoff:** external communication, authority contact, personal-data or legal questions.
- **Audit/evidence handoff:** evidence on resolutions, follow-up and management involvement.

## Typical mistakes

- Management receives only technical status reports.
- Decisions are not documented as resolutions with owner and deadline.
- Risk acceptance is created implicitly by inaction.
- Managers see information security as the task of the ISMS owner.
- Resource shortage is not openly named as a risk.
- Lessons learned remain in the security team and do not reach a leadership routine.
- Management reviews are too rare or too general for real steering.

## Fictional mini example

A fictional SaaS provider has several overdue critical vulnerabilities because a legacy system is difficult to patch. The ISMS owner brings three options into management review: short-term maintenance window, additional segmentation or accelerated replacement. Executive management decides budget for segmentation and sets a deadline for the replacement decision in the next quarter.

Evidence:

- decision paper with options,
- management minutes with resolution,
- updated risk register,
- measures log for segmentation,
- follow-up for the replacement decision.
