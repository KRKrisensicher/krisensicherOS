# A.5.6 — Contact with specialist groups and security networks

## Purpose

Contact with specialist groups and security networks ensures that an organization does not steer only from internal assumptions. It builds reliable connection points to trusted sources, industry circles, CERT/CSIRT structures, authority information, vendors, associations, or peer groups, and translates relevant inputs into risk, measure, and situation decisions.

The core is not a long distribution list, but an operated routine: Which contacts are relevant, who assesses information, when is it escalated, and which decisions result from it?

## Control objective in repository language

The organization operates traceable network and contact management for information security. Relevant external information, warnings, good practices, and experience are selectively received, assessed, distributed internally, and, where needed, transferred into risks, measures, exercises, architecture decisions, or management reviews.

## Typical risks

- If the organization has no reliable security contacts, it learns about industry-typical attack patterns, warnings, or support offers too late.
- If external notifications arrive without structure, important information is lost in mailboxes, chats, or personal contacts.
- If only individual people maintain specialist contacts, this creates dependency on informal knowledge and representation risk.
- If specialist-group information is adopted without review, false priorities or unnecessary measures may result.
- If confidential internal information is shared in networks, data protection, contractual, reputational, or security risks may arise.
- If contacts are not maintained, trusted points of contact for situation picture, coordination, or specialist assessment are missing during an event.

## Triggers

- new or changed ISMS scope, new critical service, or new industry/supply-chain dependency.
- relevant warning from CERT, CSIRT, vendor, association, authority, specialist group, or industry circle.
- security event, near miss, crisis exercise, or lessons learned.
- management question about the threat situation, regulatory development, or industry practice.
- change of ISMS owner, security role, emergency contact, or service provider.
- annual review of the contact list, source quality, and memberships.
- new cooperation opportunity or participation in a security network, working group, or information-sharing association.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **ISMS owner / security role:** is responsible for contact strategy, source assessment, internal distribution, and review.
- **Incident response or crisis role:** uses contacts for situation picture, coordination, and specialist questions during an event.
- **Specialist and asset owners:** assess whether external notifications are relevant for specific services, processes, or information.
- **Communications / PR:** reviews external communication when information from networks is to be used publicly or with partners.
- **Legal / data protection:** reviews boundaries for information sharing, confidentiality, personal data content, and contractual obligations.
- **Management:** decides on memberships, resources, participation in exchange formats, and accepted residual risks.

## Implementation

### Minimum start

Goal: maintain a small number of reliable contacts and sources so that notifications are not lost.

1. The organization names an owner for security contacts and specialist groups.
2. A small contact and source list is maintained: CERT/CSIRT information, vendor channels, industry association, trusted peer group, or service provider contact.
3. For each source, it is defined what it is used for: warning, situation picture, good practice, incident support, regulatory input.
4. Incoming relevant notifications are recorded in a simple log or ticket.
5. The owner decides whether a notification is forwarded to incident response, vulnerability management, risk review, or management.
6. Confidentiality and approval rules for external sharing are briefly documented.

Minimum evidence:

- contact and source list with owner,
- assessment note on relevant notifications,
- forwarding or ticket for measures,
- review date of the contact list,
- approval or stop rule for external information sharing.

### Solid practice

Goal: external specialist information is assessed repeatably and translated into governance routines.

1. Sources are assessed by relevance, trustworthiness, timeliness, and connection to the ISMS scope.
2. Roles and deputies for network maintenance, receipt, and triage are defined.
3. Notifications are classified: situation information, technical warning, regulatory input, industry incident, exercise finding.
4. Relevant information is connected with asset, risk, supplier, or measure registers.
5. Memberships, working groups, and exchange formats are reviewed annually for value and effort.
6. Clear rules define which internal information may be shared in external circles and which only after approval.
7. Findings flow into ISMS review, awareness, vulnerability management, incident response, or BCM exercises.

Strong evidence:

- maintained source and contact register,
- triage log for external notifications,
- measure or risk references from specialist information,
- review of memberships and information channels,
- documented approvals or rejections for information sharing,
- lessons learned from exchange formats.

### Advanced practice

Goal: specialist networks are used as part of situation picture, resilience, and decision-making capability.

1. Contact management is connected with threat intelligence, incident, BCM, and management-review routines.
2. For critical services, defined external points of contact exist: vendor, managed service, industry CERT, authority, or peer circle.
3. Relevant situation information is condensed into a short situation briefing or risk dashboard.
4. The organization deliberately participates in exercises, working groups, or industry-specific exchange formats.
5. Confidentiality levels and approval paths for shared information are trained.
6. Network quality is assessed: response time, usability, timeliness, coverage of critical topics, contribution to decisions.

## Routine flow

1. **Source or contact emerges:** new membership, vendor channel, CERT feed, industry circle, or personal specialist contact.
2. **Assess relevance:** check connection to scope, risks, critical assets, industry, and operating model.
3. **Update register:** record contact, purpose, owner, deputy, confidentiality boundary, and review date.
4. **Triage notification:** classify external information, determine urgency and affected roles.
5. **Distribute internally:** provide relevant information to asset owners, incident response, vulnerability management, BCM, procurement, or management.
6. **Derive decision:** document measure, risk adjustment, exercise, communication need, or no action.
7. **Store evidence:** save notification, assessment, distribution, and decision traceably.
8. **Review network:** check value, coverage, timeliness, and deputies at least annually.

## Decisions

- Which security networks, specialist groups, and sources are truly relevant for scope and industry?
- Which contacts are considered critical for an event?
- Who may prioritize external information internally and initiate measures?
- Which internal information may be shared in specialist groups?
- Which memberships or exchange formats justify effort and cost?
- When does an external notification require a management decision rather than operational handling?

## Evidence

### Strong evidence

- current contact and source register with owners and deputies,
- triage log of relevant notifications with decision,
- tickets or measures from external warnings,
- evidence of participation in specialist groups, exercises, or situation formats,
- confidentiality and approval rules for information sharing,
- review note on effectiveness and timeliness of contacts,
- management decision on memberships, resources, or residual risks.

### Weak evidence

- personal address books without organizational access,
- newsletter subscriptions without evaluation,
- attendance certificates without connection to risks or measures,
- unclear chat groups without owner and confidentiality rules,
- old contact lists without deputy or review date.

### Evidence gaps

- no defined point for external security information,
- no check whether notifications are internally relevant,
- no contact paths for incident or crisis cases,
- no rules for external information sharing,
- no deputy when the network owner is absent,
- no connection to risk, measure, or situation processes.

## Effectiveness review

Review questions:

- Are owners and deputies known for the most important security and situation sources?
- Can external notifications be assigned to an internal triage and decision path?
- In recent reviews, did specialist-group information lead to measures, risk adjustments, or deliberately documented non-action?
- Are confidentiality boundaries for information sharing clear?
- Are contacts reachable and current in an incident or crisis?
- Is the value of memberships and exchange formats reviewed?

Possible metrics:

- share of critical sources with owner and deputy,
- number of triaged external notifications,
- time from receipt to internal assessment,
- share of notifications with measure or risk decision,
- overdue contact reviews,
- uncovered critical topics or industry sources.

## BSIG/NIS2 connection point

Contact with specialist groups and security networks is connectable to NIS2-oriented governance, risk management, situation picture, incident capability, supply-chain connection, and management oversight. For affected organizations, the concrete connection should be assessed in the requirements register and in the situation-picture/incident routine.

This artifact does not replace legal assessment, any obligation to participate in specific groups, or a binding review of applicability.

## Boundaries

- This artifact is not a replacement for formal incident reporting paths or communication with authorities.
- It does not replace legal, data protection, or classified-information review for information sharing.
- It does not state that a membership proves security or conformity.
- It contains no licensed standards text or confidential situation information.
- It must not lead to uncontrolled sharing of internal security details.

## Handoffs

- **Threat intelligence handoff:** external notifications about attack patterns, vulnerabilities, or campaigns.
- **Incident handoff:** contact need during a security event, coordination, situation picture, or specialist question.
- **BCM/crisis handoff:** industry-specific situation, dependencies, or exercises with external bodies.
- **Legal/data protection handoff:** sharing internal information, personal data details, confidentiality, contractual obligations.
- **Communications handoff:** external statements, press/partner communication, or public reference to network information.
- **Management handoff:** memberships, resources, participation in exercises, accepted information gaps.
- **Audit/evidence handoff:** missing contact timeliness, unclear source assessment, or non-traceable triage.

## Typical mistakes

- Contacts are maintained only personally and are not available to the organization.
- Newsletters are collected but not assessed.
- Specialist groups are attended without translating findings into measures or risk decisions.
- During an event, points of contact are outdated or unreachable.
- Internal information is shared in external circles without approval.
- Management funds memberships but receives no decision-ready value report.
- The organization confuses network presence with operational security capability.

## Fictional mini example

A fictional operator of a digital specialist portal participates in an industry security circle. An attack pattern against similar portals is discussed there. The ISMS role records the notification in the triage log, assigns it to the customer portal and the vulnerability process, and asks the platform owner to check the affected component. In the next ISMS review, the organization decides to additionally include the vendor channel as a critical source.

Evidence:

- entry in the source register,
- triage note on the industry notification,
- ticket to the platform owner,
- review result for the affected component,
- review decision to expand the source list.
