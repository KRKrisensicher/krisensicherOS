# A.6.4 — Handling rule violations

## Purpose

Handling rule violations ensures that security deviations are treated fairly, traceably and effectively. The aim is not the harshest possible sanction, but a robust process that connects protection needs, clarification of facts, proportionality, learning and necessary escalation.

## Control objective in repository language

The organisation operates a clarified routine for reporting, assessing, handling and following up security-relevant rule violations by employees or external parties. The routine separates subject-matter security assessment, employment-/contract-law steps, incident handling and improvement measures.

## Typical risks

- If violations are handled informally, unequal treatment, repeated errors and missing traceability arise.
- If every violation is automatically treated as a disciplinary case, willingness to report decreases and learning opportunities are lost.
- If severe violations are not escalated, damage, insider risks or legal consequences remain untreated.
- If Incident Response and HR/Legal are not aligned, evidence, rights or protection measures may be put at risk.
- If service provider violations are not pursued contractually, external risks remain without consequence.

## Triggers

- Report or observation of a security violation.
- Incident, near miss, audit finding, control deviation or repeated non-observance.
- Misuse or improper use of access, data, devices, cloud services or communication channels.
- Violation by external staff or service providers of agreed security rules.
- Result from monitoring, review or investigation, where permitted and clarified.
- Management, HR, Legal or data protection request regarding a security-relevant fact pattern.

## Roles and responsibilities

- **ISMS owner / security role:** assesses security relevance, risk impact and necessary protection measures.
- **Manager / client owner:** clarifies context, day-to-day practice and immediate organisational measures.
- **HR / people function:** manages employee relation, fairness, communication and employment-law interfaces.
- **Legal / data protection:** reviews investigation framework, evidence, data protection, contractual and co-determination questions.
- **Incident Response:** takes over when the violation triggers a security event or suspected compromise.
- **Procurement / supplier management:** follows up violations by external parties and contractual measures.
- **Management:** decides on severe cases, exceptions, resources and cultural issues.

## Implementation

### Minimum start

Goal: rule violations are consistently recorded, assessed and escalated.

1. A reporting path for security-relevant violations is defined.
2. A simple classification distinguishes mistake, repeated deviation, gross breach of duty, suspected incident and external contractual violation.
3. Responsible roles for initial assessment, HR/Legal handoff and incident handoff are named.
4. Every handling documents facts, assessment, decision, measure and follow-up date.
5. Repeated patterns lead to training, process or control improvement.

Minimum evidence:

- reporting and escalation path,
- case or ticket log,
- classification decision,
- measure and closure evidence,
- handoff to HR, Legal, data protection, Incident Response or supplier management.

### Solid practice

Goal: handling is proportionate, repeatable and learning-oriented.

1. Categories and escalation criteria are described and usable for managers.
2. Investigations follow clarified rules on access to evidence, data protection and involvement of relevant functions.
3. Measures range from coaching, retraining and process correction to formal employment- or contract-law steps.
4. Severe cases are coordinated with Incident Response, Legal, data protection and management.
5. External violations are followed up through supplier management and contractual obligations.
6. Trends feed into awareness, access control, operating rules and management review.

Strong evidence:

- classification and escalation scheme,
- case file with decision and handoffs,
- evidence of proportionate measures,
- lessons learned and control improvements,
- management decision for severe or recurring cases,
- supplier communication for an external violation.

### Advanced practice

Goal: rule violations are used as a governance signal without losing fairness and the legal framework.

1. Case patterns are evaluated in aggregate without unnecessary personal detail analysis.
2. Recurring violations trigger root-cause analyses: unclear rule, unrealistic process, tool problem, leadership issue or deliberate circumvention.
3. Incident, HR, Legal, data protection and supplier processes are integrated with clear handoff points.
4. Managers are enabled to handle cases early, fairly and with documentation.
5. Management sees decision-ready trends: critical recurrences, process gaps, resource conflicts and cultural indicators.

## Routine flow

1. **Violation becomes known:** report, review, incident, audit or observation.
2. **Check initial protection:** limit immediate risks, such as blocking access or stopping data outflow where required.
3. **Delimit the facts:** what is known, which rules are affected, which systems or data are involved?
4. **Classify:** mistake, recurrence, severe violation, suspected incident or external contractual violation.
5. **Trigger handoffs:** involve HR, Legal, data protection, Incident Response, supplier management or management according to criteria.
6. **Decide measure:** coaching, retraining, process change, access adjustment, formal steps or contractual measure.
7. **Follow up:** document implementation, closure, follow-up date and possible recurrence.
8. **Learn:** adjust rule, training, tooling or control.

## Decisions

- Which cases may managers handle themselves and which must be escalated?
- When does a rule violation become an incident or investigation case?
- Which evidence may be used and who decides on this?
- Which measures are proportionate and effective?
- How are external violations handled contractually or operationally?
- Which patterns belong in the management review?

## Evidence

### Strong evidence

- documented case with facts, classification and decision,
- handoff evidence to HR, Legal, data protection, Incident Response or supplier management,
- measure and closure evidence,
- lessons learned with adjustment of rule, training or control,
- aggregated trend analysis,
- management decision for severe or recurring violations.

### Weak evidence

- informal email without decision or closure,
- generic warning or sanction list without security assessment,
- incident ticket without HR/Legal clarification where people are involved,
- training as a standard answer without root-cause analysis,
- statistics without categories and handoffs.

### Evidence gaps

- no uniform classification,
- severe cases without management or Legal handoff,
- evidence use without data protection clarification,
- external violations without supplier follow-up,
- recurrences without process improvement.

## Effectiveness review

Review questions:

- Are violations classified consistently and fairly?
- Are handoffs clear where people, suspected incidents and external parties are involved?
- Do measures lead to fewer recurrences or better processes?
- Are severe cases escalated in time?
- Is evidence complete but data-minimising?
- Does management recognise structural causes instead of only individual cases?

Possible metrics:

- number of cases per category,
- time to initial assessment,
- share of repeated violations,
- cases with triggered incident/HR/Legal handoff,
- open measures from rule violations,
- process improvements from lessons learned.

## BSIG/NIS2 connection point

Regulated handling of violations is compatible with NIS2-oriented governance, incident capability, cyber hygiene, security culture, supply chain management and management responsibility. Specific requirements and reporting references must be assessed organisation-specifically and by humans.

This artefact does not replace legal, employment-law or data protection advice and does not replace assessment of reporting obligations.

## Boundaries

- No legal assessment of individual violations or employment-law measures.
- No instruction to monitor or analyse personal data without clarification.
- No equation of mistakes, learning and disciplinary measures.
- No certification, conformity or security assurance.
- No ISO 27002 texts or real case data.

## Handoffs

- **HR handoff:** employee relation, leadership, measures, fairness and people process.
- **Legal/data protection handoff:** investigation framework, evidence, personal data, contractual or reporting questions.
- **Incident handoff:** suspected compromise, data outflow, misuse of access or ongoing danger.
- **Supplier handoff:** violations by external persons, contractual measures and evidence.
- **Management handoff:** severe violations, recurrence patterns, cultural problems or resource conflicts.
- **Audit/evidence handoff:** incomplete case files, missing decisions or measures not followed up.

## Typical mistakes

- Rule violations are either trivialised or immediately sanctioned to the maximum.
- Security assessment, HR process and Incident Response run uncoordinated.
- Managers handle similar cases differently.
- Personal data is collected without clarifying purpose and boundaries.
- External violations disappear into the service provider channel without evidence.
- Lessons learned do not reach training, rules or technical controls.

## Fictional mini example

A fictional business unit uses a non-approved cloud storage service to share project files. The manager reports the case to the ISMS owner. The initial assessment finds no indication of data outflow, but a repeated process violation. Data protection is involved because personal data could be affected. The measure combines deletion from the tool, approval of a suitable exchange path and a team briefing. In the management review, a decision is made to provide the official file exchange faster.

Evidence:

- case ticket with classification,
- data protection handoff,
- evidence of clean-up,
- team briefing,
- measure for the official exchange solution,
- management decision on the resource issue.
