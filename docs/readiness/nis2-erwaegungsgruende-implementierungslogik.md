# NIS2 Recitals: Implementation Logic for Readiness

Status: 2026-05-25

## Purpose

This analysis translates the recitals of the NIS2 Directive into practical implementation questions. It is not a legal interpretation and does not replace review by qualified legal advice.

The NIS2 Directive is linked here: <https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj?locale=de>

## Why the recitals matter

The recitals explain which problem the directive is intended to solve. krisensicherOS does not derive legal claims from them, but implementation principles:

- broad, cross-sector resilience instead of an isolated IT checklist,
- risk-based security measures instead of purely formal documentation,
- management responsibility instead of delegation to the technical team,
- supply chain and service provider relevance,
- notification capability and crisis communication,
- European minimum harmonization in national implementation,
- supervision, evidence capability, and effective governance.

## Implementation fields from the recitals

### 1. Uniform minimum level

Implementation question:

- Is there a shared minimum logic for risk analysis, measures, evidence, review, and management decision?

Required artifacts:

- scope and entity type check,
- risk management routine,
- measures backlog,
- evidence pack structure,
- management review routine.

### 2. Critical and important services

Implementation question:

- Which services, locations, systems, data flows, and third parties contribute to providing essential or important services?

Required artifacts:

- service/process inventory,
- dependency map,
- supplier/service provider register,
- business impact note,
- technical asset and system map as internal, non-public evidence.

### 3. Risk management as an operating routine

Implementation question:

- Is risk regularly assessed, prioritized, treated, and reviewed for effectiveness?

Required artifacts:

- risk analysis methodology,
- risk register,
- measures tracking,
- effectiveness review,
- management decision on residual risk.

### 4. Incident and notification capability

Implementation question:

- Can the organization detect, assess, escalate, and report security incidents within required timeframes?

Required artifacts:

- incident triage rule,
- notification and escalation matrix,
- role map for 24/72-hour/follow-up logic,
- communication approvals,
- exercise and lessons-learned logs.

### 5. Supply chain and service providers

Implementation question:

- Are direct providers, IT service providers, cloud/MSP/MSSP dependencies, and critical components managed?

Required artifacts:

- supplier register,
- criticality assessment,
- minimum requirements for service providers,
- evidence/review routine,
- exit/fallback considerations.

### 6. Management responsibility and training

Implementation question:

- Is there a responsible executive management routine with knowledge, decision readiness, budget logic, and prioritization logic?

Required artifacts:

- management review agenda,
- executive management training evidence,
- decision log,
- risk acceptance process,
- resource and measures decision.

### 7. Evidencability and supervision

Implementation question:

- Can the organization show which measures apply, who operates them, when they were reviewed, and what evidence is created?

Required artifacts:

- evidence pack index,
- control and routine description,
- review calendar,
- findings/remediation process,
- approval and change log.

### 8. Digital services and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690

Implementation question:

- Does the organization fall into one of the digital entity types addressed by the Implementing Regulation?

If yes, NIS2 readiness must additionally evaluate:

- technical and methodological minimum requirements for risk management measures,
- criteria for significant security incidents,
- evidence capability of risk and measures processes,
- training/awareness,
- sector- or service-specific thresholds for incident assessment.

## Practical readiness conclusion

A questionnaire alone is not sufficient for actual implementation. At minimum, the following are needed:

1. Preliminary applicability check with a legal review note.
2. Source register with NIS2, BSIG, annexes, EnWG, and Implementing Regulation.
3. Service/sector/entity type mapping.
4. Risk management and incident routine.
5. Supply chain and service provider routine.
6. Evidence pack structure.
7. Management review with decisions and training evidence.
8. Legal review by counsel or the responsible legal function of applicability and obligations.
