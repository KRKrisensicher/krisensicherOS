# Incident Escalation: Initial Assessment

## Situation / Trigger

Use when a signal, finding, or technical indicator must be classified as a possible incident.

## Goal

This playbook prepares a safe human initial assessment: what is known, what is unclear, who must decide, and what escalation is needed. Agents may structure, but must not make live incident decisions.

## Roles

- Incident owner: leads the initial assessment and decides on escalation.
- Technical specialist role: provides facts about systems, logs, timeframe, and impact.
- Communications role: reviews internal and external communication needs.
- Data protection/legal: review notification, data protection, or contractual questions if affected.
- Management/crisis team: decides in case of high impact, resource needs, or external communication.

## Input Data

- Incident signal with source and time
- affected systems, processes, or data classes
- known impacts and open assumptions
- existing incident or crisis plans
- contact and escalation paths

## Process

1. Describe the signal: source, time, affected systems, and known facts.
2. Separate uncertainties from confirmed observations.
3. Preliminarily classify the impact on availability, integrity, confidentiality, and operations.
4. Hand over escalation needs, notification checks, and communication needs to human roles.
5. Define the next 30 to 60 minutes: owner, tasks, deadline, renewed situation point.

## Escalation

Escalate if:

- legal or data protection assessment is needed,
- risk acceptance or budget decision is needed,
- critical evidence is missing,
- roles or owners remain unclear,
- external communication is being prepared,
- live incident or crisis decisions are affected.

## Outputs

- Review or workshop note
- completed templates
- action backlog
- Evidence Needs List
- Decision Log
- Lessons Learned
- handoff to appropriate agents, skills, or human roles

## Follow-up

- Define actions with owner and deadline.
- Update the Decision Log.
- Add evidence pack or register entries.
- Set the next review cadence.
- Add lessons learned to the improvement list.

## Boundaries

- does not provide legal advice
- does not provide data protection advice
- does not provide certification or compliance assurance
- no management decision by agents
- no live crisis leadership
- no real customer, personal, or confidential organizational data in public examples
