# Set up a Minimum Viable ISMS

## Situation / Trigger

Use when an organization wants to make an initial ISMS operational without immediately building a complete documentation system.

## Goal

The playbook defines scope, roles, risk routine, initial controls, evidence, and review cadence. It creates an operable starting point, not a certification assurance.

## Roles

- ISMS owner: confirms scope, review cadence, and open management questions.
- Risk owner: assess risks and decide on treatment proposals in their area.
- Control owner: describe routine, trigger, evidence, and escalation point.
- Evidence reviewer: reviews evidence and gaps.
- Management: decides risk acceptance, resources, and priorities.

## Input data

- Organizational or service scope
- critical information assets, processes, and service providers
- initial risk list or known findings
- existing policies, controls, or evidence
- management objectives and boundaries of the initial scope

## Process

1. Define scope and out-of-scope for the initial ISMS state.
2. Record the most important risks and affected owners.
3. Describe a small number of starter controls with trigger, process, evidence, and review date.
4. Hand over open risks, acceptance questions, and resource needs to management.
5. Define review cadence, evidence pack, and next expansion stage.

## Escalation

Escalate if:

- legal or data protection assessment is needed,
- risk acceptance or budget decision is needed,
- critical evidence is missing,
- roles or owners remain unclear,
- external communication is being prepared,
- live incident or crisis decisions are affected.

## Outputs

- Review or workshop note
- completed templates
- action backlog
- Evidence Needs List
- Decision Log
- Lessons Learned
- Handoff to appropriate agents, skills, or human roles

## Follow-up

- Define actions with owner and deadline.
- Update the Decision Log.
- Add evidence pack or register entries.
- Set the next review cadence.
- Add Lessons Learned to the improvement list.

## Boundaries

- does not provide legal advice
- does not provide data protection advice
- does not provide certification or compliance assurance
- no management decision by agents
- no live crisis leadership
- no real customer, personal, or confidential organizational data in public examples
